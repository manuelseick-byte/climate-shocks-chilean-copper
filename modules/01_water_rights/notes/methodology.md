# Methodology and scope

## Purpose

This module provides institutional and descriptive context for the broader analysis of climate shocks and adaptation in Chilean copper mining. It is not designed as a standalone causal evaluation of Chilean water policy.

## Water-rights processing

Regional DGA registry files were harmonised and translated into a common schema. Flow measures were standardised where units permitted conversion. A curated mapping of legal holder names was used to identify rights associated with the mining operations covered by the project.

## Water-use trends

COCHILCO annual statistics were used to construct national and regional summaries of mining water extraction by source. Where the statistical yearbook structure changed over time, the scripts identify the relevant tables and harmonise the overlapping series.

## Geospatial analysis

DGA Mapoteca layers were combined with the project mine registry to locate major copper operations relative to aquifer restriction/prohibition zones and exhausted river basins. These maps describe spatial exposure to governance constraints. They do not establish that those constraints caused subsequent investment or production responses.

## Adaptation indicators

Mine-level desalination status was compiled from project source material and combined with the governance maps to provide descriptive evidence on how water sourcing differs across operations.

## Limitations

- Granted water rights are legal entitlements and do not measure actual withdrawals.
- Mine/company matching relies partly on curated legal-name mappings.
- Geospatial proximity to a restricted water body is an exposure measure, not a causal treatment.
- Desalination commissioning dates and status can differ across sources and should be interpreted as project-level classifications.
- The public repository omits large raw datasets and intermediate workbooks.
